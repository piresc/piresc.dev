# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Backend engineers, hiring managers, and collaborators visiting to evaluate technical credibility and working style. They arrive from LinkedIn, GitHub, or a referral link — scanning quickly, deciding in seconds whether to read deeper or reach out.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that communicates engineering depth and craft sensibility without saying either out loud. The site itself is the proof: if the portfolio is well-built, debuggable, and considered, it implies the same about the person behind it. Success looks like a visitor who leaves with a clear sense of who piresc is and what working with them would be like.

## Brand Personality

Quiet confidence. Restrained, precise, unhurried. The work speaks; the presentation doesn't compete with it. Technical competence expressed through craft rather than declared in copy.

## Anti-references

- Generic dev portfolios: template-driven, GitHub-readme-as-a-site, default dark themes with no point of view.
- Corporate/enterprise: stiff, formal, LinkedIn-profile energy. Buzzword-heavy, visually safe, interchangeable.

## Design Principles

1. Show, don't declare — let craft choices (motion, spacing, typography) communicate skill without self-congratulatory copy.
2. Earned restraint — every element present because it carries weight, not because the template had a slot for it.
3. Pacing over density — give content room to breathe; a visitor should feel unhurried, not overwhelmed.
4. Systems thinking visible — the portfolio's own architecture (tokens, components, consistent motion) should reflect the systems mindset described in the work.
5. Substance over signaling — real impact metrics and clear descriptions over inflated language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance. Keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion alternatives for all animation, sufficient color contrast (4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text/UI). Already partially implemented — maintain and extend.
